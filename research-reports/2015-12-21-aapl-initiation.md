# Apple Inc. (AAPL): Initiation

**PFW-SIF Equity Research · Information Technology team**
**Published:** December 21, 2015 · **Rating:** Buy · **Price at pitch:** ~$107 · **12-month target:** $135
**Investment committee:** Approved, December 22, 2015 · **Executed:** January 4, 2016 at $105.35

> Facts in this report are limited to what was public on the publication date. Prepared for the PFW-SIF student investment fund (simulated portfolio). Educational only; not investment advice.

## Recommendation
We recommend initiating a position in Apple. The market is pricing Apple as if the iPhone has peaked and the company is a declining hardware maker. We think that view ignores how loyal the installed base is, how much cash the business throws off, and how fast the Services line is growing on top of that base.

## The business today (fiscal 2015, ended September 2015)
- Revenue of **$233.7 billion**, up 28%; net income of **$53.4 billion**; diluted EPS of **$9.22**.
- **231 million iPhones** sold, a record, driven by the larger-screen iPhone 6 and 6 Plus.
- Greater China revenue rose 84% to about **$59 billion**.
- Services (App Store, iTunes, iCloud, AppleCare, Apple Pay) reached about **$20 billion**, larger than many Fortune 500 companies on its own.
- Cash and marketable securities of about **$206 billion**.

## Why we would buy now
1. **Valuation.** At roughly $107, Apple trades near **11.5x trailing earnings**, below the S&P 500 multiple, and lower still once its net cash is subtracted. That is the price of a no-growth business.
2. **Installed base and switching costs.** Hundreds of millions of active devices tied together by iOS, iCloud and the App Store make customers unusually sticky. Each upgrade cycle starts from a larger base.
3. **Services growth.** Services grow with the installed base, not with unit sales, and carry higher margins than hardware. Investors are paying almost nothing for this.
4. **Capital return.** Apple pays $0.52 a quarter (about a 2% yield) and has a $200 billion capital return program through March 2017. Buybacks steadily shrink the share count.

## Risks
- **iPhone 6s cycle.** December reports of supplier order cuts suggest the iPhone 6s will not match last year's record cycle; fiscal 2016 iPhone units could decline.
- **China.** A slowing Chinese economy and currency weakness could hit the fastest-growing region.
- **Dependence on one product.** iPhone is about two-thirds of revenue.
- **New categories unproven.** Apple Watch has not yet shown it can be another major product line.

## Valuation and target
Applying a 13x multiple to our estimate of normalized earnings of about $9 per share, plus net cash of roughly $15 per share, gives about **$135**, or ~26% upside before dividends.

## What would make us sell
Two consecutive years of a shrinking installed base, a cut to the capital return program, or Services growth falling below the growth of the device base.

## Committee vote
Approved, December 22, 2015. Position sized at one quarter of the fund's starting capital, bought at the close on the first trading day of 2016.
