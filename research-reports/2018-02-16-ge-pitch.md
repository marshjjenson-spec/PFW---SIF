# General Electric Company (GE): Turnaround pitch (not approved)

**PFW-SIF Equity Research · Industrials team**
**Published:** February 16, 2018 · **Rating:** Buy · **Price at pitch:** ~$15 · **12-month target:** $19
**Investment committee:** Not approved, February 22, 2018

> Facts in this report are limited to what was public on the publication date. Prepared for the PFW-SIF student investment fund (simulated portfolio). Educational only; not investment advice.

## Pitch summary
GE's shares had fallen about 50% in a year. The team argued that a new CEO, a reset dividend and a plan to focus on Aviation, Power and Healthcare made it a contrarian buy with franchise assets priced at a discount.

## Key facts at the time
- **John Flannery** became CEO in August 2017 and began a portfolio review, with up to $20 billion of planned divestitures.
- The **dividend was cut in half** to $0.12 a quarter in November 2017.
- In January 2018 GE took a **$6.2 billion after-tax charge** for its legacy long-term-care insurance book. GE Capital expects to set aside about **$15 billion** over seven years. The SEC opened an investigation into the accounting.
- 2018 guidance: adjusted EPS of $1.00–$1.07. **GE Aviation** remained the strongest unit.

## Bull case presented
- Aviation's installed base of jet engines produces decades of high-margin service revenue.
- Healthcare could be worth more on its own.
- Sentiment was extremely negative, so any stabilization could move the stock.

## Why the committee declined
- **Balance sheet uncertainty.** Insurance reserves, pension deficits and GE Capital's debt made the downside hard to size.
- **Power weakness.** The gas turbine market was in a deep downturn, with no clear bottom.
- **Accounting questions.** The SEC investigation added risk the team could not evaluate.
- **Better use of capital.** At the same meeting, the committee preferred Amazon's growth profile.

The fund tracks this pitch's return against the S&P 500 from the pitch date, so the decision can be judged over time.
