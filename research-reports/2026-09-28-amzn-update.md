# Amazon.com, Inc. (AMZN): Position review, why we continue to hold

**PFW-SIF Equity Research · Consumer Discretionary team**
**Published:** September 28, 2026 · **Rating:** Hold · **Held since:** February 26, 2018

> Prepared for the PFW-SIF student investment fund (simulated portfolio). Returns are total returns (dividends reinvested) from the purchase date through October 2, 2026, from Tiingo end-of-day data. Educational only; not investment advice.

## Result so far
Amazon has returned about **+231%** since purchase, slightly ahead of the **+217%** S&P 500 Total Return over the same period. It is the fund's weakest relative performer.

## How the original thesis played out
- **AWS** grew into a business of more than $100 billion a year and remains the largest cloud provider. Its growth slowed in 2023 as customers cut costs, then picked up again with AI demand.
- **Advertising** became one of Amazon's most profitable businesses, as the 2018 pitch expected.
- **Retail margins** were hurt by the pandemic overbuild of fulfillment capacity in 2021–2022. A cost reset and a regionalized delivery network have since improved them.

## Why we hold
- The three engines from the original thesis (AWS, advertising and Prime retail) are all larger and more profitable than in 2018.
- The main debate is AI capital spending. Amazon is investing heavily in data centers and its own AI chips (Trainium), and has a large investment in Anthropic. That spending lowers free cash flow today but positions AWS for AI workloads.
- The stock has been flat for long stretches since 2020, so its valuation is more reasonable relative to earnings than when we bought.

## What would change our view
AWS losing share to Microsoft Azure or Google Cloud in AI workloads, or capital spending that fails to earn adequate returns.
