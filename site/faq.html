Questions about the fund

The fund

Is this real money?

Not yet. PFW-SIF currently manages a simulated (paper) portfolio. Every price and dividend is real market data, and trades are recorded at actual closing prices, but no real money is invested. The site labels this clearly, and it will switch to "Fund performance" if the fund begins managing real assets.

Why does the track record start in 2016?

The fund reconstructs a historical record, starting with a $10,000 portfolio in January 2016, so students can study a full market cycle. Reports written for that period use only information that was public at the time, and every page that shows it says so.

What does the fund invest in?

U.S.-listed common stocks, with cash held for new ideas. The fund does not use leverage, short selling, options or cryptocurrency. See the investment policy.

Who decides what to buy and sell?

Students. A sector team pitches an idea, and the investment committee votes on it. The faculty advisor attends but does not vote. See the Team page.

Why does one position exceed the policy limit?

Positions can grow past the limit when they rise faster than the rest of the portfolio. The fund does not sell automatically; the committee reviews the position and decides whether to trim. The live status of each limit is on the Investment process page.

The numbers

How are returns calculated?

The portfolio is valued at every market close from the trade ledger and closing prices, with dividends credited on the ex-date. Monthly returns use the Modified Dietz method, which adjusts for cash added or withdrawn during the month. Longer periods compound the monthly returns. Full details are in the Methodology section of the Performance page.

What is the benchmark?

The S&P 500 Total Return, measured with the SPY ETF's adjusted closing prices so that dividends are included. A price-only index would make the fund look better than it is.

Where does the data come from?

Daily prices, dividends and splits come from Tiingo. Company financial figures in the research reports come from the companies' SEC filings. The data updates every weekday evening after the market close.

Why are rejected pitches shown?

Showing only the winners would hide whether the committee is any good at choosing. The Research page compares the ideas the committee approved with the ones it declined, both measured against the S&P 500 from the pitch date.

How do I join?

Applications open at the start of each semester and are open to students in any major. See Join the fund.
