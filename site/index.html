Methodology & dataHow these numbers are produced Show methodologyHide methodology
Return calculation

Portfolio values are calculated each trading day from the fund’s trade ledger: cash plus every position valued at that day’s closing price. Dividends are credited as cash on the ex-dividend date and stock splits adjust share counts. Commissions and fees recorded in the ledger reduce the portfolio value.

Monthly portfolio returns are calculated from month-end portfolio values. In months with no external cash flows the return is the simple change in value. In months with contributions or withdrawals, the Modified Dietz method weights each flow by the share of the month it was invested.

R = (EMV − BMV − CF) / (BMV + Σ wᵢ · CFᵢ)

Monthly returns are chained geometrically, so the series approximates a time-weighted return. Growth of $100,000 compounds those monthly returns from the start of the selected window; cumulative arithmetic sums are never plotted.

Benchmark

The benchmark is the S&P 500 Total Return, represented by SPY adjusted closes with dividends reinvested. SPY's returns are net of the ETF's small expense ratio, so they run marginally below the index itself. Benchmark returns are measured over exactly the same dates as the portfolio, starting from the close on the fund’s inception date; a month missing from either series is excluded from both and disclosed under data quality.

Risk statistics

All statistics use aligned monthly returns over the selected window and require at least 12 observations. Standard deviations are sample standard deviations.

Annualized return = Π(1 + rₜ)^(12/n) − 1 Volatility = stdev(rₜ) × √12 Sharpe = (Annualized return − Rf) / Volatility Sortino = (Annualized return − Rf) / Downside deviation Downside deviation = √mean(min(rₜ − Rf/12, 0)²) × √12 Beta = cov(rₚ, r_b) / var(r_b) Jensen's alpha = 12 × [ mean(rₚ − Rf/12) − β × mean(r_b − Rf/12) ] Tracking error = stdev(rₚ − r_b) × √12 Information ratio = (Ann. return₍ₚ₎ − Ann. return₍b₎) / Tracking error Capture (up/down) = Ann. return₍ₚ₎ / Ann. return₍b₎ over months where r_b > 0 (or < 0)

Active return is the difference between annualized portfolio and benchmark returns. It is not alpha: alpha adjusts for the portfolio's beta and the risk-free rate.

Drawdown & rolling figures

Drawdown is the value at each month-end divided by the highest prior value in the window, minus one. The maximum drawdown is the deepest such point. Recovery is the first month-end at or above the prior peak. Rolling 1-year return compounds the trailing 12 monthly returns.

Data quality

Missing months are never interpolated or filled. A statistic that cannot be calculated reliably is shown as N/A with the reason. Simulated, backtested and live results are labeled separately and are never combined into one track record.

Past performance does not guarantee future results. Simulated performance does not represent actual trading or actual assets under management and may not reflect the impact of market factors such as liquidity, commissions and taxes.
