Membership

Learn investing by doing it, in front of a committee.

Members research real companies, write reports, pitch to the investment committee and help manage the fund's portfolio. You do not need to be a finance major or have investing experience; you need curiosity, reliability and a willingness to have your work questioned.

What you get

Why students join

Real analyst work

Financial modeling, valuation, industry research and written reports, the core skills of equity research and asset management jobs.

A public track record

Your reports are published on this site with their price targets and results, something concrete to show employers.

Presentation practice

Pitching to the committee and defending a thesis under questioning builds the skills interviews test.

Leadership

Members can move up to sector lead and officer roles that run the portfolio, risk and operations.

Expectations

What members do

Every week

  • Attend the weekly fund meeting (about one hour).
  • Work with your sector team on coverage and research (two to four hours).
  • Follow news on the companies your team covers or holds.

Every semester

  • Contribute to at least one initiation report or position review.
  • Present a pitch or review to the investment committee.
  • Take part in the annual review of the holdings your team covers.

How to apply

Four steps

Apply

Start of each semester

Submit a short application: your major and year, why you want to join, and which sectors interest you. A résumé is welcome but not required.

Stock idea

One page

Write one page on a company you think is worth owning or avoiding: what it does, why the market might be wrong about it, and one risk. Clear thinking matters more than polish.

Conversation

About 20 minutes

Talk with two fund officers about your idea and your interests. There are no trick questions and no finance quiz.

Onboarding

First two weeks

New members join a sector team, learn the fund's report template and valuation model, and shadow a committee meeting before presenting.
