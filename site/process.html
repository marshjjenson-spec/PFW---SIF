How we invest

A repeatable process, written down before the money moves.

PFW-SIF invests in a concentrated portfolio of U.S.-listed companies the students understand well. Every position starts as a written report, is approved by a committee vote, is sized within policy limits, and is reviewed against its original thesis. This page is the fund's investment policy statement in plain language.

Philosophy

What we believe

Quality at a reasonable price

We look for companies with durable competitive advantages, strong cash generation and management teams that allocate capital well, bought when the market price leaves room for error.

Long holding periods

Our edge is patience and a written thesis, not trading. We expect to hold positions for years and judge them on whether the thesis played out, not on the last quarter.

Know what would make us wrong

Every report states the conditions under which the fund would sell. Writing them down in advance keeps decisions honest when a stock falls or rallies.

Measure everything

Returns are reported against the S&P 500 Total Return with dividends included, and declined pitches are tracked too, so the committee's judgment is measured, not assumed.

The process

Seven steps from idea to exit

Idea generation

Sector teams

Each sector team screens its part of the market for companies with strong economics trading below what the team believes they are worth. Ideas enter the pitch pipeline on the Research page at the Researching stage.

Research

Sector team

The team studies the company's filings, competitive position, management and industry, builds a financial model, and values the company with a discounted cash flow and comparable-company multiples.

Initiation report

Sector team

The team writes a report with a recommendation, a 12-month price target, the investment thesis, catalysts, risks and the conditions that would make the fund sell. See every report.

Committee pitch and vote

Investment committee

The team presents to the investment committee and answers questions. The committee votes; a simple majority approves. Every vote, approved or not, is recorded and published.

Execution and sizing

Portfolio manager

Approved positions are bought at the next market close. The position size is set within the policy limits below. Every trade is entered in the fund's ledger with its rationale and a link to the report.

Monitoring

Sector team · risk officer

Holdings are valued every trading day. Each position gets a written review at least once a year, and the risk officer checks the portfolio against policy limits on the Holdings page.

Exit

Investment committee

The fund sells when the thesis breaks, when a stated sell condition is met, when the price reaches a level that leaves little upside, or to stay within policy limits. Every exit gets a short exit memo.

Investment policy statement

Objective, benchmark and limits

Limits come from the fund's live settings, and the status column compares them with today's portfolio.

Objective

Long-term growth of capital, aiming to beat the S&P 500 Total Return over rolling five-year periods after costs.

Benchmark

S&P 500 Total Return, measured with SPY with dividends reinvested.

Universe

U.S.-listed common stocks and ETFs. No options, short sales, leverage or margin. No cryptocurrency.

Risk management

How the fund controls risk

Diversification

Position and sector limits keep any one company or sector from dominating results. Positions that grow past the limit through gains are reviewed for trimming.

Cash discipline

The fund stays nearly fully invested, holding enough cash to fund new ideas without trying to time the market.

Independent checks

A risk officer outside the pitching team reviews concentration, beta, drawdowns and tracking error against the benchmark every month.

Pre-written sell rules

Each report lists what would make the fund wrong. The annual review tests the position against those conditions.

Record keeping

Every trade, rationale and vote is recorded in the ledger, so results can be rebuilt and audited from the source data.

No conflicts

Members disclose personal holdings in a company before pitching it and do not trade it personally around a committee vote.
