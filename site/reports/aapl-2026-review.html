Investment Thesis

The thesis worked; now the question is position size

Apple has returned about 1,309% since we bought it, against 356% for the S&P 500 Total Return. The 2015 thesis was that a loyal installed base and fast-growing Services were being valued as a declining hardware business. That has largely played out.

Services became a business of more than $100 billion a year with gross margins far above hardware. Heavy buybacks substantially cut the share count, so earnings per share grew faster than revenue.

We keep Apple at Hold. The ecosystem and cash generation are intact, but the valuation is far above the 11.6x earnings we paid. Growth now depends more on Services and on whether Apple delivers competitive AI features.

Catalysts

  1. AI featuresDelivering useful on-device and cloud AI features could restart device upgrade demand.
  2. Services growthSubscriptions, payments and advertising continue to grow faster than hardware.
  3. Capital returnContinued buybacks support earnings per share.
  4. New categoriesWearables and new device types add to the installed base.

Risks to the Thesis

  1. App Store regulationUS court rulings and EU rules threaten App Store fees, a key source of Services profit.
  2. AI competitionRivals may move faster on AI assistants and features.
  3. ValuationA premium multiple leaves less room for disappointment.
  4. ConcentrationAt about 41% of the fund, Apple dominates the portfolio's risk.

Why we continue to hold Apple

What went right

Our 2015 pitch said the market was valuing Apple's ecosystem like a declining hardware maker. Since then, the installed base grew, Services became a large high-margin business, and buybacks shrank the share count. The multiple investors pay rose from about 11x to a premium to the market.

Why not sell

None of our exit conditions has been met. The installed base has not shrunk, the capital return program continues, and Services is still growing. In a real account, selling would also realize a very large taxable gain.

Why not add

At today's price, we no longer have the margin of safety we bought with. Most of the upside from re-rating has already happened, and future returns depend mostly on earnings growth.

Position size

Apple has grown to about 41% of the fund through appreciation, above the fund's 10% single-position guideline. The committee will consider trimming to reduce concentration risk, with proceeds kept in cash or used for new pitches.

What would change our view

A sustained slowdown in Services, a forced change to App Store economics, or evidence that AI features are moving upgrade demand to rival platforms.
