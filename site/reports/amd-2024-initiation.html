Investment Thesis

The second source for AI accelerators

We recommend initiating a position in AMD after a roughly 40% pullback from its March 2024 high. Data center revenue more than doubled year over year in Q2, and MI300 accelerator sales passed $1 billion in a single quarter for the first time.

AI buyers want an alternative to Nvidia for supply, pricing and negotiating leverage. MI300X is the first product to offer one at scale, and AMD has committed to a new accelerator every year.

The early-August market selloff took AMD to about $130. That gives us a better margin of safety on a business whose data center segment is now its largest.

Catalysts

  1. MI325X and an annual cadenceNew accelerators launching in late 2024 and 2025 keep AMD competitive on memory capacity and performance.
  2. Hyperscaler deploymentsLarge cloud customers are expanding MI300X deployments for AI inference.
  3. EPYC share gainsServer CPU share gains continue on performance per watt.
  4. PC recoveryAI-capable Ryzen PCs and an aging installed base support a client recovery.

Risks to the Thesis

  1. Nvidia's software leadCUDA is deeply entrenched. AMD's ROCm software must keep improving for customers to switch.
  2. Supply constraintsAdvanced packaging and high-bandwidth memory capacity could limit shipments.
  3. Export controlsRestrictions on AI chip sales to China limit the market.
  4. VolatilityThe stock trades at a premium multiple and swings with AI sentiment.

Why we are buying AMD

Customers want a second source

AI accelerators are the most supply-constrained part of the data center. Buyers who depend on one supplier pay higher prices and wait longer. AMD's MI300X gives them an alternative, and large customers have publicly committed to deploying it.

A proven execution record

Under Lisa Su, AMD went from near bankruptcy to taking significant server CPU share from Intel. That record of delivering roadmaps on time gives us confidence in its annual accelerator cadence.

Data center is now the largest segment

At $2.8 billion a quarter and growing over 100%, data center is changing AMD's mix toward higher margins and more durable demand.

A better entry price

The pullback from March and the early-August selloff mean we are buying about 40% below the recent high, without any break in the fundamental story.

What would make us wrong

Customers pausing MI300 orders, AMD missing its annual product cadence, or gross margins falling as it discounts to win share.
