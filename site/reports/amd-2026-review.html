Investment Thesis

The second-source thesis played out

AMD has returned about 387% in just over two years, against 51% for the S&P 500 Total Return. It is the fund's best performer relative to the market.

Our 2024 thesis was that AI buyers wanted a credible alternative to Nvidia. That happened: large customers signed multi-year accelerator agreements, including OpenAI's October 2025 commitment to deploy AMD Instinct GPUs at multi-gigawatt scale.

AMD kept its annual accelerator cadence with the MI325, MI350 and MI400 series, and bought ZT Systems to design complete AI server racks. EPYC server CPUs kept gaining share.

Catalysts

  1. MI400 rack-scale systemsFull AI racks compete directly with Nvidia's integrated systems.
  2. Customer deploymentsMulti-year agreements begin shipping in volume.
  3. EPYCServer CPU share gains continue as AI data centers also need general-purpose compute.
  4. SoftwareROCm improvements lower the cost for customers to switch.

Risks to the Thesis

  1. AI spending cycleA pause in hyperscaler spending would hit AMD harder than our other holdings.
  2. NvidiaNvidia remains the leader, with a faster product cadence and deeper software ecosystem.
  3. ExecutionRack-scale systems are more complex to deliver than chips.
  4. ValuationAt nearly five times our cost, much of the success is priced in.

Why we continue to hold AMD

What went right

Our 2024 pitch argued that customers wanted a second source of AI accelerators and that a 40% pullback offered a good entry. Both proved correct: AMD won large multi-year commitments, and the stock nearly quintupled.

Why not sell

The long-term opportunity in AI data centers remains large, and AMD is now an established supplier rather than a hopeful one. None of our exit conditions has been met.

Why not add

The price now reflects much of the success. At nearly five times our cost, new money would buy much less margin of safety, and AMD is the holding most sensitive to a slowdown in AI spending.

Position size

AMD has grown from about 3% of the fund's capital at purchase to about 16% of the fund. That is above the 10% guideline, and the committee will review it alongside the Apple trim.

What would change our view

Customers delaying accelerator deployments, AMD falling behind Nvidia's product cadence, or gross margins falling as competition intensifies.

DCF cross-check

Our five-year DCF gives $371.37 per share, 41% below the $633.91 price. The market price assumes faster or longer growth than our base case. That gap is a main reason we hold rather than add. A DCF is sensitive to its inputs, so we use it as a check on the target, not the target itself.
