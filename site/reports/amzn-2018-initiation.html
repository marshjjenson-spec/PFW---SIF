Investment Thesis

AWS, advertising and the Prime flywheel

We recommend initiating a position in Amazon. Investors still judge Amazon on thin reported earnings. We think it is really two leading businesses, a global retail platform and the largest cloud provider, with a third, advertising, now growing quickly.

In 2017, Amazon Web Services grew 43% to $17.5 billion of revenue and earned $4.3 billion of operating income, more than the whole company earned. Advertising and other revenue grew about 58% to $4.7 billion.

Amazon reinvests most of its cash flow in fulfillment, video and new markets, so earnings understate its earning power. Valued on segment profits and free cash flow, the stock is easier to justify than its P/E suggests.

Catalysts

  1. AWS margin disclosureEach quarter AWS shows operating leverage as revenue scales faster than costs.
  2. Advertising growthSponsored product ads monetize high-intent shopping traffic with very little added cost.
  3. Whole Foods integrationPrime benefits in Whole Foods stores and grocery delivery extend Amazon into a large new category.
  4. International progressLosses in India and other markets should narrow as those businesses scale.

Risks to the Thesis

  1. ValuationAbout 100x trailing earnings leaves little room for disappointment.
  2. SpendingHeavy investment could keep margins low longer than expected.
  3. Regulation and politicsAmazon's size in retail draws growing political scrutiny.
  4. Cloud competitionMicrosoft Azure and Google Cloud are investing heavily to catch up with AWS.

Why we are buying Amazon

Two category leaders in one stock

Amazon is the largest online retailer in the US and the largest cloud infrastructure provider in the world. Either business alone would be a reasonable investment. Together, they share technology, data and customers.

AWS is early

Most enterprise computing still runs in companies' own data centers. As those workloads move to the cloud over the next decade, AWS's lead in breadth of services and scale should let it capture the largest share and grow margins as it does.

Advertising changes the margin story

Shoppers search Amazon with the intent to buy, which makes its ads valuable. Advertising carries far higher margins than retail, so its growth lifts the profitability of the whole company.

The Prime flywheel

Prime members buy more, more often. Each new benefit, from video to faster shipping to Whole Foods discounts, improves retention and gives Amazon more data to improve the service.

What would make us wrong

AWS growth falling below 25% with no margin expansion, or spending that keeps free cash flow flat for several years. Either would suggest the reinvestment is earning poor returns.
