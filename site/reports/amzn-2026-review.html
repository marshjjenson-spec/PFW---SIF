Investment thesis

Three engines bigger than ever; AI spending is the debate

Amazon has returned about 231% since we bought it, slightly ahead of the 217% S&P 500 Total Return. It is the fund's weakest relative performer, mainly because of the 2021–2022 overbuild in fulfillment capacity and slower cloud growth in 2023.

The three engines from our 2018 thesis are all larger and more profitable: AWS is a business of more than $100 billion a year, advertising is one of Amazon's most profitable lines, and retail margins have recovered after a cost reset and a regionalized delivery network.

The current debate is AI capital spending. Amazon is investing heavily in data centers and its own Trainium chips, and has a large investment in Anthropic. That lowers free cash flow today but positions AWS for AI workloads.

Why we continue to hold Amazon

What went right

AWS and advertising grew as our 2018 pitch expected, and both now produce most of Amazon's profit. The Prime flywheel kept customers loyal through the pandemic and after.

What went wrong

Amazon nearly doubled its fulfillment network during the pandemic and then had too much capacity as demand normalized. Cloud customers also cut costs in 2023. Both weighed on the stock for several years.

Why we hold

Those problems have been addressed: retail margins recovered, and AWS growth is picking up with AI demand. The stock's relative underperformance has made its valuation more attractive.

The AI bet

Amazon's heavy AI spending is the key risk and the key opportunity. If AWS captures a large share of AI workloads, today's spending will look cheap. We are watching AWS growth and free cash flow closely.

What would change our view

AWS losing share in AI workloads, or several years of capital spending without a recovery in free cash flow.
