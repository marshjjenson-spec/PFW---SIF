Investment thesis

A contrarian bet on a turnaround

The team recommended General Electric after a roughly 50% decline in a year. A new CEO, a reset dividend and a plan to focus on Aviation, Power and Healthcare could make it a contrarian buy.

John Flannery became CEO in August 2017. He cut the dividend in half, announced about $20 billion of divestitures and opened a review of the company's structure. GE Aviation, with a large installed base of jet engines, remained a high-quality franchise.

The team argued that sentiment was so negative that any stabilization in Power or clarity on insurance liabilities could lift the shares.

Catalysts

  1. Portfolio reviewA decision on separating businesses, such as listing Healthcare, could unlock value.
  2. Aviation strengthCommercial aircraft engine demand and service revenue continue to grow.
  3. Power stabilizationCost cuts in the gas turbine business could stop the earnings decline.
  4. DivestituresAsset sales would reduce debt and simplify the company.

Risks to the thesis

  1. Insurance reservesGE took a $6.2 billion after-tax charge in January 2018 for its long-term-care insurance book and expects to put about $15 billion into reserves over seven years.
  2. Power downturnDemand for gas turbines is in a deep slump, with no clear bottom.
  3. SEC investigationThe SEC is investigating GE's accounting for insurance reserves and long-term service contracts.
  4. Balance sheetPension deficits and GE Capital's debt make the downside hard to size.

Why the committee passed on GE

The bull case presented

Aviation's installed base produces decades of high-margin service revenue, and Healthcare could be worth more on its own. Sentiment was at a historic low.

Downside too hard to size

Insurance reserves, pension deficits and GE Capital's debt meant members could not estimate how bad the worst case might be.

No floor in Power

The gas turbine market was still deteriorating, and Power was a large share of industrial profits.

Accounting questions

An open SEC investigation added a risk the team could not evaluate.
