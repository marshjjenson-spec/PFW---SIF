Investment Thesis

Mobile search, YouTube and a new era of cost discipline

We recommend initiating a position in Alphabet Class A shares. Search is moving to mobile without losing its economics. Paid clicks grew about 23% last quarter, and YouTube is taking brand advertising from television.

The October 2015 reorganization into Alphabet separates the core Google business from long-shot projects such as self-driving cars and life sciences. New CFO Ruth Porat has brought cost discipline, and the company announced its first share buyback, about $5.1 billion.

Starting with Q4 2015 results, Alphabet will report Google and Other Bets separately. We expect that to show how profitable the core business is once billions of Other Bets losses are stripped out.

Catalysts

  1. Segment reportingQ4 2015 results, due in early February, will be the first to show Google and Other Bets separately.
  2. Mobile monetizationMobile search ads and new formats on YouTube should close the price gap between mobile and desktop ads.
  3. Cost disciplineSlower headcount growth and capital spending targets under the new CFO should lift margins.
  4. BuybacksThe first repurchase program signals a willingness to return cash. Future programs could be larger.

Risks to the Thesis

  1. RegulationThe European Commission's Google Shopping antitrust case is ongoing, and fines or remedies are possible.
  2. Other Bets spendingThese projects lose billions a year, and the timing of any payoff is uncertain.
  3. Falling ad pricesCost-per-click keeps declining as traffic shifts to mobile and emerging markets.
  4. Strong dollarAbout half of revenue comes from outside the US, so currency moves reduce reported growth.

Why we are buying Alphabet

Search is the best business on the internet

Search ads reach people at the moment they want something, so advertisers pay for intent rather than impressions. Google handles the large majority of searches in most markets, and Android and Chrome keep its distribution secure as usage moves to phones.

Mobile is additive, not a threat

The bear case says mobile ads earn less than desktop ads. But mobile is increasing the number of searches, and paid clicks are growing faster than at any point in the past few years. Lower prices per click are offset by much higher volume.

YouTube is the next television

YouTube is the leading destination for online video. As brand budgets move from TV to online video, YouTube is positioned to capture a large share, with years of growth ahead.

Alphabet adds discipline

The holding-company structure makes management accountable for the core business's margins and for the cash spent on Other Bets. The first buyback and a new CFO from Wall Street show that the company now thinks about shareholder returns.

What would make us wrong

Sustained declines in paid-click growth, regulation that restricts how Google ranks its own services, or Other Bets losses that keep growing without any milestones.
