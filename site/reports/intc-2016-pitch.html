Investment Thesis

Data center and Altera to offset a shrinking PC market

The team recommended Intel as a cash-rich leader whose data center business was growing while the market focused on falling PC sales.

The Data Center Group earned $4.1 billion of revenue in Q3 2015, up 12%. Intel holds over 95% of the server CPU market. The $16.7 billion Altera acquisition, closed in December 2015, adds programmable chips used in networking and cloud data centers.

At about 13x earnings with a nearly 3% yield, the team argued the shares priced in permanent PC decline and nothing for data center growth.

Catalysts

  1. Cloud build-outHyperscale cloud providers are buying servers at a record pace, nearly all of them Intel-based.
  2. Altera integrationCombining FPGAs with Xeon processors opens new data center products.
  3. Memory and IoTNew memory technology and Internet of Things chips add growth options.
  4. Capital returnDividend increases and buybacks are supported by about $20 billion of operating cash flow a year.

Risks to the Thesis

  1. PC declineOver half of revenue still comes from PCs, a market that fell about 10% in 2015.
  2. Mobile failureIntel spent billions subsidizing tablet chips and still has no position in smartphones.
  3. Manufacturing scheduleThe 10nm process is late, which could narrow Intel's manufacturing lead.
  4. CompetitionARM-based server chips and a resurgent AMD could pressure data center share.

Why the committee passed on Intel

The bull case presented

A dominant server CPU franchise, a 3% dividend yield and a low multiple. The team saw Intel as a value stock with a hidden growth business.

Too much PC exposure

Members thought data center growth would struggle to offset a PC business that was more than half of revenue and shrinking.

Manufacturing risk

Delays at 10nm raised the question of whether Intel's manufacturing lead, its key competitive advantage, would hold.

Opportunity cost

With limited capital, the committee preferred Alphabet's faster growth, which was pitched at the same meeting.

How we track it

The fund records the return of every declined pitch against the S&P 500 from the pitch date, so the committee's judgment can be measured over time.
